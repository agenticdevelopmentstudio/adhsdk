
Process Exit Watch tells a caller that a child process has exited, by the one route that always arrives: a termination handler installed on the process before it is launched. It exists because the obvious route does not always arrive. The source's own comment records that on macOS 26.6 a blocking wait-until-exit call can miss its wake-up for a child that exits within a few milliseconds, and park its thread for good: about one call in five against `/usr/bin/true`, and about one credential round in a thousand against `security`. The child is already reaped by then, so a timeout that polls `isRunning` never fires either. The termination handler, posted by the same reaping, was not seen to miss in 500 runs where the blocking wait missed 89. One missed wake-up held a credential round open for good and froze every later account switch behind it.

The type offers three things: installing the watch (`init`), a bounded wait (`waitForExit(timeout:)`), and an ending sequence (`stop(_:)`): terminate, a grace, kill, a second grace. `CommandRunner` is the whole-output form built on it. A caller that owns the process's streams (a pty transcript it scans as it arrives, or a stdout passed straight through) uses this type directly. See the sibling recipe for the command runner by its UUID in `related`.

It is a final class, `ProcessExitWatch`, declared `@unchecked Sendable`, in the core framework.
